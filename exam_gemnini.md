# State Exam Questions - Answers

Here are short, beginner-friendly, and meaningful answers to the exam questions to help you prepare.

## Questions on the scientific specialty

**1. What was the Stanford prison experiment and why is it a warning to future researchers?**
It was a psychological study in 1971 where college students played roles of "guards" and "prisoners" in a fake prison. It had to be stopped early because the "guards" became abusive and the "prisoners" suffered emotional trauma. It is a warning because it shows how quickly experiments can become unethical and cause real psychological harm if not strictly controlled.

**2. Name at least 3 informatics-related areas, which can be considered exponential technologies. Explain your choice.**
1. **Artificial Intelligence (AI)**: It learns and improves rapidly, boosting automation across all industries.
2. **Cloud Computing**: It provides easily scalable computing power and storage, allowing companies to grow without buying expensive physical hardware.
3. **Internet of Things (IoT)**: The number of connected devices is growing exponentially, creating massive amounts of data for smart cities and automated industries.

**3. Provide a brief description of a Markov Chain.**
A Markov Chain is a mathematical system that transitions from one state to another. Its main rule is that the next state depends *only* on the current state, not on the sequence of events that preceded it. (It's like a board game where your next move depends only on where you are now, not how you got there).

**4. Can a study which is approved by an institutional ethics board still be unethical? Explain.**
Yes. An ethics board reviews the plan *before* the study starts. However, during the study, unexpected harm might occur, researchers might deviate from the approved plan, or new information might emerge making the continuation of the study unethical.

**5. Give a legal evaluation of the following situation. University published your full name and phone number in the list of Hackathon winners. Can you ask to remove the data? Why?**
Yes, you can ask to remove the data. Under data protection laws (like GDPR), personal data like phone numbers shouldn't be published without explicit consent, as it violates your right to privacy. You have the "right to be forgotten" or the right to withdraw your consent.

**6. What is the Chomsky Hierarchy?**
It is a classification of formal languages and grammars in computer science. It divides grammars into four levels (from Type 0 to Type 3), where each level is more restricted than the previous one. It is fundamental in designing programming languages and compilers.

**7. What is informed consent?**
It is the process of getting voluntary permission from a participant before they join a study or undergo a medical procedure. The person must be fully informed about what will happen, the risks, and the benefits, and they must agree without any pressure.

**8. Imagine you created a novel approach (technology). How can you protect your rights? Which of them will belong to University?**
You can protect your rights by applying for a patent, registering a copyright, or keeping it a trade secret. If you created it using the University's resources (like labs or funding) or during your studies there, the University usually owns the commercial/financial rights, but you retain the moral right to be named as the creator/inventor.

**9. Explain the reasons to organize the description of computer networks in layers, like the ISO/OSI.**
Layering breaks down the complex process of networking into smaller, manageable parts. It creates standardization, allowing different hardware and software to communicate. It also means that if you change one layer (e.g., upgrading a physical cable), you don't have to change the other layers (e.g., the web browser).

**10. What is the h-index and how is it calculated? What problems can occur in using this index to rank researchers?**
The h-index measures a scientist's productivity and impact. An h-index of "h" means the researcher has published "h" papers that have each been cited at least "h" times. 
**Problems:** It favors older researchers (who had more time to gather citations), varies greatly between different scientific fields, and ignores the context of citations (e.g., a paper cited because it is wrong still increases the h-index).

**11. Basis of logical operations. Logic gates. Implement a NAND gate with transistors. Then implement NOT and OR gates with NAND.**
Logic gates (AND, OR, NOT) are the basic building blocks of digital circuits.
*   **NAND with transistors:** Two transistors placed in series (one after the other), but inverted so it outputs 0 only when both inputs are 1.
*   **NOT from NAND:** Connect both inputs of the NAND gate together.
*   **OR from NAND:** Put a NOT gate (made from NAND) on each of the two inputs, and feed those into a third NAND gate.

**12. What is the role of SQL in DB Management Systems?**
SQL (Structured Query Language) is the standard language used to communicate with relational databases. Its role is to allow users to create, read, update, and delete data, as well as to manage the structure (tables and relationships) of the database itself.

**13. What is the Vancouver protocol?**
It is a standard style for formatting references and citations, mostly used in medical and scientific papers. It uses a numbered system where references are numbered in the text sequentially, and the full details are listed in numerical order at the end of the document.

**14. What is an NDA (non-disclosure agreement)? Did you sign one with University? What does it say?**
An NDA is a legal contract where parties agree to keep certain information confidential and not share it with outsiders. As a student or researcher, you might sign one if you work on sensitive, unreleased projects. It usually states what information is secret, how long it must be kept secret, and the penalties for leaking it.

**15. What is a definite integral?**
In calculus, a definite integral calculates the exact area under a curve on a graph between two specific points (limits). It results in a specific number (an area), unlike an indefinite integral which results in a general formula.

**16. Explain how domain name services (DNS) work.**
DNS acts like the phonebook of the internet. When you type a human-readable website name (like google.com), DNS translates it into a computer-readable IP address (like 192.168.1.1) so your browser can connect to the correct server.

**17. How would you maximize CPU utilization of very big matrix multiplication using high-speed full mesh network connection among multicore CPU-based machines?**
You would divide the giant matrices into smaller chunks (blocks). You distribute these chunks across all the machines using the high-speed network. Each multicore CPU works on multiplying its specific chunks in parallel. The results are then sent back over the network and combined to form the final matrix.

## Questions on the history and philosophy of science

**1. Theories of information society.**
These theories suggest that we have moved from an industrial economy (based on manufacturing goods) to an information economy. In this society, the creation, distribution, and manipulation of information and knowledge are the most important economic and cultural activities.

**2. Computer science as an interdisciplinary science.**
Computer science isn't just about programming; it connects with many other fields. It uses math for algorithms, psychology for user interfaces, biology for bioinformatics, and linguistics for AI. It acts as a tool to solve complex problems across almost every other discipline.

**3. The problem of artificial intelligence and its evolution.**
The main challenge is how to make machines think, learn, and make decisions like humans. Its evolution started with simple rule-based programs (expert systems), moved to machine learning (learning from large amounts of data), and is now focused on deep neural networks handling complex tasks. A major ongoing problem is ensuring AI acts safely and ethically.

**4. Computer and information ethics.**
This is a branch of ethics dealing with how computing professionals should behave. It covers issues like user privacy, data security, copyright (software piracy), bias in algorithms, and the moral responsibility of developers if their software causes harm.

**5. The use of mathematical modeling in various fields of knowledge.**
Mathematical modeling uses math formulas to represent real-world systems. It is used in physics to predict motion, in economics to forecast market trends, in biology to model the spread of diseases, and in engineering to test structures before building them physically.

## Questions on pedagogical activities

**1. The specifics and limitations of online education. Opportunities and pedagogical design in the implementation of online education in higher education. Give a detailed answer.**
*   **Specifics & Opportunities:** It offers a flexible schedule, is accessible from anywhere, and allows students to learn at their own pace.
*   **Limitations:** It lacks face-to-face social interaction, requires high student self-discipline, can be affected by technical issues, and makes practical/lab work difficult to conduct.
*   **Pedagogical Design:** Online courses must be designed differently than regular lectures. They require highly interactive elements (quizzes, forums), clear structured instructions, shorter video modules to maintain attention, and varied assessment methods to ensure active learning.

**2. Psychological and professional characteristics of IT specialists and their development in the pedagogical process in higher education. Give a detailed answer.**
*   **Characteristics:** IT specialists need strong logical and analytical thinking, high concentration, continuous learning ability, and problem-solving skills. While they often work independently, they also need strong communication and teamwork skills for large projects.
*   **Development in Education:** Universities should not only teach coding but also actively develop soft skills. This is achieved through team-based projects, hackathons, presentations, and teaching students how to adapt to rapidly changing technologies rather than just memorizing one specific tool.
